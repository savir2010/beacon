Cost of Conflict Worksheet

What agreeing now and going to court are each likely to cost you, side by side. You supply every number, and you can change any of them to see how the picture moves.

For the mediator

Keeping it neutral

  • Introduce the worksheet at the first session as something every client completes, so no one feels singled out later.
  • Complete it for both parties, ideally in separate caucus, and let each party supply their own numbers.
  • Encourage each party to get a real fee estimate from a consulting attorney and enter that instead of a placeholder.
  • Offer no view on how a judge would rule. The odds belong to the party.
  • Use Presentation mode to hide this panel when the screen is shared.

Questions to ask while you work

  1. What makes you confident about those odds? What would a judge hear from the other side?
  2. If you won exactly what you’re asking for, what would change in your day-to-day life?
  3. Which of these costs surprised you?
  4. What would an agreement need to include for you to feel it was fair?
  5. How do you want your children to describe this period of their lives years from now?
This worksheet is an educational tool for the parties’ own decision-making. It is not legal, tax or financial advice and does not predict how a court will rule. Results depend entirely on the figures entered. The mediator is a neutral and does not represent either party. Each party is encouraged to review their options with an independent attorney. Figures in the example are illustrations, not estimates for any case. Nothing entered here is saved: closing or reloading the page clears it.
